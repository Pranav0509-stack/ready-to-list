Number 1 · the chance a hearing moves the case

pₖ, from the organisers' own failure data

pₖ = (1 − process still pending) × (1 − (absence + not ready + other))
  • Start: the measured rate for the hearing type and the real reasons hearings fail.
  • Last order: "Await warrant" or "Absent: Accused" multiplies that risk by 2.5, rescaled so each type's average still matches the data.
  • Bounds: at least 0.02; total risk capped at 0.98.
LeverEffect
Process trackingPending process → 0
Confirmation 2 days beforeNot ready × 0.5
Fixed slot, advocate groupedAbsence × 0.65

Check: with the levers off it reproduces the case study, 60 listed, 20 heard, 10 moving forward.

Number 2 · expected minutes

eₖ: plan for the average, keep 5% spare

eₖ = pₖ × mₖ × 1.063 + (1 − pₖ) × 2 + 1
PartMeaning
mₖThe organisers' minutes: admission 5, appearance 10, plea 15, evidence 30
1.063Average overrun: eσ²/2, σ = 0.35
2Calling and adjourning a case
1Calling the next case
  • Worked example: evidence, 30 min, 50% chance → 0.5 × 31.9 + 0.5 × 2 + 1 = 17.9 min.
  • Why lognormal: a hearing can't take less than zero but can run long.
  • Why the average: about 18 hearings are summed, so long and short cancel out.
  • σ is assumed: the data has no measured durations yet.

Number 3 · the value of listing a case today

vₖ: priority actually delivered

vₖ = (Sₖ + bₖ + cₖ) × pₖ
PartMeaning
SₖSamay score, 0 to 100: age 35, readiness 25, near disposal 15, churn 15, urgency 10
bₖListing boost: 0 first time, +10 second, +25 from the third
cₖ+15 while under 180 days: NI Act s.143, trial within six months
pₖThe chance it moves today

A case that deserves time but will not go ahead today only wastes the slot.

The solve, per sitting

Rules first, then CP-SAT on what is left

1 · Bail, always→ 2 · 4+ year cases, up to 25% (locked)→ 3 · CP-SAT on the remaining room R→ 4 · Next 5 on standby
choose xₖ ∈ {0, 1} maximise Σ vₖ × eₖ0.75 × xₖ subject to Σ eₖ × xₖ ≤ R

Whole numbers for CP-SAT: minutes × 10, value × 1,000 (0.1-minute precision). Top 300 candidates, 8 workers, 1-second cap. One court day: 83 ms.

The one design choice to defend

Why value × minutes0.75

ObjectiveRanks per minute byWhat happens
Σ vₖvₖ / eₖOnly short matters. Evidence and arguments starve; cases never reach judgment.
Σ vₖ × eₖvₖLength ignored. One long hearing pushes out three good short ones.
Σ vₖ × eₖ0.75vₖ / eₖ0.25Mild length penalty. Short matters preferred, long hearings that dispose still compete.

0.75 is a tuning choice, not fitted. One line to change; test 0.5, 0.75 and 1 before a live rollout.

Why a knapsack, and why CP-SAT

Sorting leaves gaps. CP-SAT finds the best fit.

40 minutes left:

CaseWorthMinutes
A9035
B6020
C5520

Sort: A only, worth 90, 5 min wasted.
CP-SAT: B + C, worth 115, day full.

  • Knapsack is the right shape: one judge, one case at a time. The order doesn't change the minutes used, so the hard part is which cases.
  • CP-SAT: in our earlier test it proved the best answer in under 0.1 s; a linear-programming solver was still 5% short at 30 s.
  • Free and open source, Apache 2.0, ready for DRISTI 2.0.
