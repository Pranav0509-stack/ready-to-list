A day in Court 12

Four cheque-dishonour cases under NI Act s.138, and exactly how Samay decides which are heard, when, and why.

Justice Sehgal's docket, from the organisers' real data. Scores are real; chances and minutes are rounded for the example.

1
The idea in one line

Every case gets two numbers

Worth

How much hearing it today delivers

Its priority score times the chance the hearing actually goes ahead.

Minutes

How long it will take

Full time if it goes ahead, about 2 minutes if it is adjourned, 1 minute to call it.

The optimiser picks the cases worth the most that fit in the judge's time.

How the score is built · five factors, added

Sₖ = 35·age + 25·readiness + 15·stage + 15·churn + 10·urgency

FactorMaxValue, 0 to 1Where the number comes from
Age35min(1, years since filing ÷ 8)8 = this docket's mean age + 2 sd, computed once
Readiness25type rate × (0.7 + 0.3 × share of needed people present)Type rate from the organisers' table; people from the last order's "Present:" line
Near disposal15stage number ÷ 1011 stages, admission 0 to judgment 10
Churn15min(1, hearings held ÷ expected − 1)Expected = median hearings summed over stages so far
Urgency10"last chance" 1.0, "for judgment" 0.8, else 0Read from the last order

Added, never multiplied: an old case at a slow stage is not pushed to zero. Each case is scored on its own file; the score never compares cases.

Beside the score

Status, flags, and what the judge may change

Status and flags, from the last order

  • Conditional: "return of summons", "issue NBW", "take steps" and the like. Listed only when the process is back.
  • Last chance, Churning, Backlog 4y+, Ageing 5y+, Liberty lane for bail, Mediation report pending.
  • Flags never change the score; they steer the day-builder.

Weights the judge can move

  • Five sliders on the Priority page; the docket re-ranks live.
  • Age can never fall below 20. Everything else rescales to 100.
  • Locked and not on any slider: bail first, 25% of every sitting for 4+ year cases.

The next date follows what happened, never the calendar

gap = G(next stage) if the hearing moved the case = F(reason) if it failed = max(F(process), days until the warrant is due back) if waiting on process = 1 sitting day if the court did not reach it, or the judge was on leave

Step 7 · turning days into a real date

Calendar days become sitting days, and the date is a floor

due = today + max(1, round(gap × 5/7)) in sitting days skip weekends, Kerala HC holidays, judge's leave
Casegap× 5/7Due
ST/1166/20231410 sittingsabout 2 weeks
ST/608/201775 sittings1 week
ST/7/2025max(21, 30)21 sittingsthe warrant's return day
Not reached11 sittingtomorrow
  • 5/7: a court sits about 5 days in 7.
  • A floor, not a promise. On that day the case joins the due pool. If it does not fit, it stays due and competes tomorrow with a higher value. It is never pushed 60 days out.
  • Held cases wait for their cause: a summons still out, or a 3rd-listing failure not yet cured.

Why this is fair to litigants

Four protections built in

Old cases cannot be buried

Age carries the most points, 35, and 25% of every sitting is locked for cases over 4 years.

New cheque cases are not forgotten

+15 while under 6 months, because s.143 asks for trial within six months.

A case that keeps failing rises

+10 on the 2nd listing, +25 from the 3rd, once the reason it failed is fixed.

Nobody is called for nothing

A case waiting on a warrant or summons is not listed until it returns.

Said openly

What we measured, what we assumed

From the organisers' data

  • How often each hearing type moves a case
  • Why hearings fail, by type
  • Minutes and next-date gap per type
  • Case ages, stages and the last order's text

Our assumptions

  • Hearings vary about 35% around the table's minutes
  • The last order's warning raises that risk 2.5 times
  • 5% of the day kept spare, 25% for old cases

If the day runs over, unreached cases go to the next sitting day. If it runs short, standby is called. Every outcome the court master records turns an assumption into this court's own number.
