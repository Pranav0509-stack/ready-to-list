In three steps

Score every case. Pack the day. Give everyone a time.

1

Score

Every case out of 100: how old, whether its people will come, how close to judgment, how often it came back, whether the court said "last chance".

2

Pack

Fill each sitting like a bag: the cases most likely to move forward, until 95% of the minutes are used.

3

Time

A clock time and a one-hour window for every party, and a next date set by what happened, never a flat 60 days.

Built only on the organisers' real data: 100 cheque-dishonour cases and their reference tables, scaled to a 3,000-case docket.

Two different questions

The score decides who gets in. Grouping decides the order.

Who is listed today?

The priority score and the optimiser. Only cases likely to move forward, and only as many as the minutes allow.

In what order are they called?

Sitting, then one hearing type at a time, then one advocate at a time. Not the score: once listed, 97% are heard anyway.

The optimiser · Google OR-Tools CP-SAT

For every ready case, one yes or no: list it today?

value = (score + listing boost + s.143 boost) × chance the hearing moves the case maximise Σ value × minutes0.75 so that Σ expected minutes ≤ 95% of the sitting
1 · Bail, always→ 2 · Cases over 4 years, up to 25% of minutes (locked)→ 3 · CP-SAT picks the rest→ 4 · Five on standby

Minutes0.75 stops the day filling with only five-minute matters, so a 30-minute evidence hearing still competes.

Why this optimiser

It finds the best list, fast, and it is free

Right shape

A yes or no per case under a minutes limit is exactly the problem CP-SAT is built for.

Best, not good

In our earlier test it proved the best list in under 0.1 s; a linear-programming solver was still 5% short at 30 s.

Beats sorting

A sorted list overflows with long hearings or leaves the afternoon empty. The optimiser balances both.

Open source

Apache 2.0. Goes straight into DRISTI 2.0 with no licence.

  • Expected minutes = chance it goes ahead × minutes × 1.063 + chance adjourned × 2 + 1. Evidence at 50%: 17.9 min.
  • Clock time = sitting start + 15 min opening + the cases before it. So the day opens at 10:45.
  • Window: from the half hour before, for one hour. Expected 11:16 gives 11:00 to 12:00.
  • Runs over: to the next sitting day. Runs short: standby is called.
