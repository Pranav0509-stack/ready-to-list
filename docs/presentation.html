What we built

One flow, from the docket file to the next date

Upload the docket→ Registry pre-filing→ Readiness→ Which day (CP-SAT)→ What time (CP-SAT)→ Judge approves→ Court master, one tap→ Next date

For the judge

A draft list that fits the day, a reason for every case, and the cost of any change before approving.

For the court master

One tap per outcome; the next date follows from what the case needs, with tasks sent to both sides.

For advocates and litigants

A one-hour window instead of a whole day, with an advocate's matters grouped together.

Dashed steps use information from outside the list: new filings and each case's readiness. Everything else is the registry's own scheduling.

Prioritisation and optimisation

A score for every case, then value per minute

FactorPointsFrom
Case age35Filing date; full at 8 years for this roster
Hearing readiness25The type's real progress rate, less up to 30% for absent required people
Disposal proximity15Position in the 11 stages
Hearing churn15Hearings held against the median expected
Court-set urgency10"Last chance", "for judgment" in the last order
  • Every case scored on its own details, 0 to 100. Same score in a roster of 100 or 3,000.
  • Bail always listed locked; a quarter of each sitting for 4+ year cases locked; age weight never below 20 locked.
  • CP-SAT packs each sitting to 95% of its minutes by score x chance of moving forward, per minute.
  • 1st, 2nd and deferred listings treated differently; a deferred case is held until its last failure is cured.

Worked example from the data: ST/1261/2017, 9.2 years old, reserved for judgment, both advocates present, 43 hearings against 26 expected: 91.3.

The product · Samay

What the judge sees, what the court master does

Today

The two sittings on a clock, then the cause list: time, case, hearing, listing chip (1st, 2nd, deferred), score, advocate, and why it is listed today. Remove, restore, approve.

Calendar

A month grid with every date: sittings with their case count, holidays, judge's leave, weekends. Click a day to see its list, minutes and how many of the judge's own cases it carries.

Cases and Priority

Every case with its score, status and flags, its last order and history. Five weights the judge can move, age never below 20, the docket re-scored live.

Court master

A card per judge. Add any files or a folder; Samay recognises the docket and the reference tables by their columns. Call each matter, record the outcome; the next date follows by rule.

Justice Sehgal's docket is the organisers' roster, loaded from the repository. Every number on every screen is computed from it.

What Indian judges need beyond this

Built for a magistrate's cheque docket; the same engine for every bench

A link judge on leave days

Bail and other liberty matters go to the link judge the same day; the rest move to the next sitting, never 60 days out.

Process return from the field

Summons and warrant status from the process server and e-summons receipts, so "awaiting process" stops being guessed from the order sheet.

Confirmation by WhatsApp

Two days before, counsel confirms in one tap; parties receive their one-hour window in English and the local language.

Per-bench configuration

Sitting hours, blocks by hearing type, leave register, weights and locked rules in one file per judge; a High Court bench adds its own hearing types and minutes.

Old files, summarised

A verified cover sheet for cases over four years, so the bench does not spend the hearing re-reading the record.

Outcomes that teach the model

Each recorded outcome recomputes the rates; the lever strengths stop being assumptions.

12
The model · complexity level L3

Measured rates, read per case, solved by CP-SAT

  • Per hearing type: the real chance a hearing moves the case (plea 90%, warrant 14%), and the real mix of reasons it fails, from the organisers' data.
  • Per case: the last order is read. "Await warrant", "Absent: Accused" or "not ready" multiplies that risk 2.5 times, with each type's average held at its measured rate.
  • Per listing: 1st, 2nd and deferred listings of the same purpose are treated differently; a deferred case is held until its last failure is cured.
  • No machine learning trained on 100 cases. As the court records outcomes, the same rates are recomputed nightly.
LevelWhat proves it
L1 fixed rulesBail first, ageing quota, readiness gate, CP-SAT packing
L2 realistic dataReal success and failure rates per type, lognormal minutes, per-lever ablation
L3 behaviourParties respond to fixed slots, clustering, confirmation and short notice; outcomes feed the next plan

Lever strengths not in the data are stated assumptions in config, each switched off in turn to show its worth.

Built to go into DRISTI 2.0

One judge now, every court next

Scales by configuration

The unit is one judge's docket. A court's sittings, hearing flow, priorities and registry checks are YAML files. A new bench is a new file, not new code.

Takes the data as it is

The organisers' CSVs in, unchanged; upload a CSV or Excel docket and the app checks it and plans it.

Open and offline

Python, OR-Tools CP-SAT, scikit-learn, SQLite, Streamlit. MIT licence. No cloud AI calls.

To plug in: feed summons and warrant status and advocates' confirmations from DRISTI, run the planner each evening, and show the judge the draft for approval.
