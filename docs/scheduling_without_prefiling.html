PUCAR Scheduling Justice · Ready-to-List

What scheduling alone can do for a judge's day

If we drop the pre-filing check and every input from the parties, and keep only what the registry controls when it builds the cause list, how far does that get us? We tested it on the hackathon's own data.

The answer

Scheduling alone nearly doubles disposals. On the organisers' data, fitting the list to the minutes and setting real next dates takes 60-day disposals from 191 to 343, against 368 with everything on, and cuts wasted listings by 84%. Readiness information (whether summons and warrants are back, counsel's confirmation, the last hearing's note) mainly raises how often a heard case moves forward: 66% with scheduling alone, 82% with everything. Over a year, pre-filing matters for new complaints: 89% get past cognizance, against 4% without it.

Why scheduling alone works

Three registry moves carry most of the gain

List only what fits

Today 60 cases are listed and the court reaches 44% of them. The rest wait all day and go home. Adding up each case's expected minutes and stopping at 95% of the day brings reach to 97%.

Spend minutes where hearings succeed

Each case is scored by its priority times its chance of moving forward, per minute. Types that rarely succeed today, such as warrant returns (13.5%) and reports (8.3%), stop crowding out evidence and judgments.

A real next date

The gap comes from the next purpose in the organisers' table (5 to 45 days), not a flat 60. Cases come back when there is something to hear.

The cost of dropping readiness

Without knowing whether a warrant is back, the scheduler can only judge each type by its average. All warrant-return cases wait longer, including the ones that were ready. Heard cases move forward 73% of the time rather than 82%, and wasted listings are 360 rather than 220.

The two optional boxes are what this test switched off. Everything from priority onwards is the registry's scheduling.

  1. Priority. Each case gets a score from its hearing type, how long it has waited and how ready it is. Bail and other liberty matters come first, always.
  2. Prediction. For each case: the chance both sides appear, the chance the hearing moves the case forward, and the expected minutes. Adjourning still costs about 2 minutes.
  3. Which day. CP-SAT picks the best set of cases for each day across all three courts. A quarter of each day is locked for cases over 4 years old. Nothing is listed past 95% of the day's minutes.
  4. What time. CP-SAT sets exact start times. No advocate is due in two courtrooms at once, with 10 minutes to walk between them, and similar cases sit together.
  5. Human decision. The judge sees the list with a reason for each case, adds or removes cases and sees the cost first, then approves.
  6. Feedback. One tap records the outcome. The next date follows, and the models learn from what happened.

The cost of waiting grows over time and jumps at legal deadlines. Bail doubles at 60 days, when default bail becomes available.

Pre-filing

Where the pre-filing check matters, and where it does not

High Court filings

A petition with an unsigned vakalatnama or a short court fee fails in court. On our synthetic High Court (3 courts, 5 days), switching the check off cut effective hearings from 219 to 187 (−15%), whatever the solver. The check is never a gate: a defective filing is still accepted and only moves down the queue.

The organisers' trial court data

These are cheque-dishonour complaints, and the registry's scrutiny matters most on limitation: 96 of the 100 cases went through delay-condonation hearings (3.4 hearings each, 29% moving the case). Over a year with new complaints arriving, computing limitation at e-filing and hearing condonation with admission takes new complaints past cognizance from 4% to 89%, and halves delay-condonation listings (175 to 85).

The practical plan: ship scheduling first, since it needs only the registry's own data and gives most of the gain. Then add readiness as the court's systems expose process status, starting with warrant and summons returns.
